About me

Hi, I'm Kartiki. I am a researcher working at the intersection of security and privacy, and I also explore the HCI side of these problems.

I am a PhD student in the Computational Science Joint Doctoral Program at San Diego State University and the University of California, Irvine, co-advised by Prof. Joann Chen (PAL Lab) and Prof. Zhou Li (DSP Lab).

My research investigates privacy, surveillance, and accountability in online and emerging technologies through empirical measurement studies and user-centered evaluations. I use large-scale measurements and survey-based studies to characterize attribution and data practices, quantify privacy and transparency risks, and examine how users' perceptions and expectations shape trust in surveillance technologies and their governance.

Before my PhD, I earned an MS in Management Information Systems (specialization in Cybersecurity Management) at SDSU and worked as a Management Summer Intern at the City of San Diego.

Research interests

My research interests lie at the intersection of privacy, surveillance, technology accountability, and human trust in emerging technologies. I am particularly interested in understanding how people perceive and interact with technologies that collect, track, or influence user data, and how transparency and accountability can shape trust in these systems.

My work has included studying attribution discrepancies and deceptive practices across browser extensions, as well as investigating residents' perceptions, awareness, and expectations surrounding surveillance technologies and local surveillance policies. More broadly, I am interested in developing and evaluating technologies that are more transparent, privacy-conscious, and accountable to the communities they affect.

Some of the areas I work on:

  • Usable security and security awareness
  • Secure systems
  • Privacy-enhancing technologies, including differential privacy
  • Scams and how they can be prevented systemically

About me

Hi, I'm Kartiki — an interdisciplinary researcher working at the intersection of security and privacy, while also exploring the human-computer interaction (HCI) side of these problems.

I am a Ph.D. student in the Computational Science Joint Doctoral Program at San Diego State University and the University of California, Irvine, co-advised by Prof. Joann Chen (PAL Lab) and Prof. Zhou Li (DSP Lab). My work explores topics across the security and privacy space, including browser security, surveillance technology, and differential privacy.

My current research investigates cookie hijacking in browser extensions. Through measurement studies and user studies, I examine both how these mechanisms work and how users perceive them.

Before my Ph.D., I earned an M.S. in Management Information Systems (specialization in Cybersecurity Management) at SDSU and worked in the public sector as a Management Summer Intern at the City of San Diego.

Research interests

Usable Security & Awareness

How users understand — and misunderstand — the security risks they face.

Secure Systems

Building and assessing systems with security designed in from the start.

Privacy-Enhancing Technologies

Differential privacy and its application to real-world data analytics.

Scam Prevention

Understanding how scams work and how they can be prevented systemically.
